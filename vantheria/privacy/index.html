Privacy

Short version: no tracking, no ads, no accounts. Only your Arena champion is shared.

Last updated: October 27, 2026

What we collect

Vantheria does not track you, and never sells your data. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no third-party tracking in the app. The one thing the game shares is your Arena champion, described below.

Your saves

Your game saves are stored on your device. If you're signed in to iCloud and have the game's iCloud sync turned on, they're also stored in your own private iCloud storage so they can sync between your devices. Neither the developer nor anyone else can access them. You can delete a save at any time from the game's Settings, and deleting the app removes the saves on that device.

The Arena

In the Arena, other players fight a snapshot of your hero, and you fight theirs. When you open the Arena while signed in to iCloud, the game uploads your champion to Apple's CloudKit service: your hero's name, class, level and appearance, their stats, skills, gear and bonuses, and your Arena rating. Other players of Vantheria can see all of this. Your champion is labelled with a random ID the game makes for it. Apple also records which iCloud account created it, but only as an anonymous ID; neither other players nor the developer see your Apple ID, name or email. Pick a hero name you're happy for others to see. Names that break the game's filter are shown as a generic name instead.

If you aren't signed in to iCloud, your champion isn't uploaded; you can still fight other players'. If you report a champion, the game sends that champion's ID and the reason (its name or its build), and only the developer can read reports. To have your champion removed, email the address below.

Game Center

If you use Game Center, your leaderboard scores (the Spire, the Gauntlet and Hardcore) and achievements are handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy.

Children

The game asks for no personal information. A child's hero name and build can be seen by other players in the Arena, so we suggest children use a made-up hero name.
